## Correlation & Relationships

- Across the full `2001-2025` year-over-year dataset, the correlation between revenue growth and net-margin change is `0.5192`.
- In the top-quartile revenue-growth periods (threshold `0.4498`), net margin expanded in `4` periods, contracted in `2`, and was flat in `0`.
- This suggests a moderate positive relationship, which is consistent with some operational leverage rather than full independence.

- When net margin contracted, EPS still rose in `6` periods: `2006-2007, 2007-2008, 2010-2011, 2011-2012, 2015-2016, 2018-2019`.
- In `6` of those periods, revenue contribution alone outweighed the combined drag from margin and shares.
- That means revenue growth has historically been strong enough, in a meaningful number of periods, to still produce positive EPS growth even during margin pressure.

- For years with falling share count, the buyback contribution correlates `-0.5750` with revenue contribution and `-0.2377` with net-margin contribution.
- Based on that comparison, the EPS lift from lower share count aligns more strongly with revenue than with net margin.

# Phase 7: FCF Factors and Six-Factor Analysis

## Method Note

- Phase 7 repeats the EPS factor work for Free Cash Flow per share, using the same Model.csv annual block and the same exact log-contribution method.
- FCF identities: `FCF/S = Rev/S x FCFMargin` (two-factor) and `FCF/S = Revenue x FCFMargin / Shares` (three-factor). FCF margin = `Free cash flow / Revenue`.
- "Multiplicative addition" matches the formulas-sheet convention: the per-period log contributions add up to the total FCF/S log change (they tie out to `<1e-12`).
- Ranking is by contribution magnitude. The new **weight** is the absolute-contribution share: `weight_i = |c_i| / sum_j |c_j|` (always positive, sums to 100% within a period).
- Positive contribution helps FCF/S; negative hurts it. Share count is signed so buybacks read positive.
- The `2013-2014` share-basis break affects FCF/S exactly as it does EPS and is the main caveat for the long-history windows.
- All Phase 7 validation checks PASS (`outputs/phase7/phase7_fcf_validation_results.csv`): row counts, annual identity tie-out, exact two- and three-factor log tie-outs, input positivity, share-count directionality, weight-sum, and the six-factor join.

- Most consistent positive FCF contributor: `revenue` (positive in all 5 periods).
- Most volatile factor: `fcf_margin` (largest standard deviation; it even turned net-negative on average over the window).
- Factors moved in opposite directions and offset FCF/S movement in: `2021-2022`, `2023-2024`, `2024-2025`.

| Factor | Positive Periods (of 5) | Mean | Std Dev |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| revenue | 5 | 0.1583 | 0.1069 |
| fcf_margin | 2 | -0.0510 | 0.1303 |
| share_count | 5 | 0.0233 | 0.0083 |

This is the key contrast with the EPS rolling result: for EPS, net margin was the volatile factor but stayed a net positive over 2020-2025; for FCF, the margin factor was both the most volatile and a net drag, because rising capex compressed FCF margin even while net margin held up.

### Correlation & Relationships (FCF)

- When revenue grew significantly: across `2001-2025` the correlation between revenue growth and FCF-margin change is `0.0698` (essentially independent, versus `0.5192` for net margin). In the top-quartile revenue-growth years (threshold `0.4498`), FCF margin expanded in `4` and contracted in `2`. This suggests FCF margin is driven more by the capex cycle than by revenue scale, i.e. weaker operating-leverage signal in cash terms than in accounting terms.
- When FCF margin contracted, FCF/S still rose in `4` periods, and in all `4` of those revenue contribution alone outweighed the combined drag from FCF margin and shares. So revenue growth has repeatedly been strong enough to carry FCF/S through cash-margin pressure.
- For buyback years, the share-count (buyback) contribution correlates `-0.5750` with revenue contribution and `-0.4731` with FCF-margin contribution. The buyback lift aligns more strongly with revenue than with FCF margin, the same conclusion reached for EPS.

## Part B - All 6 Factors (EPS set + FCF set together)

The two identities share `Rev/S`/`Revenue` and `Share Count` but differ in their margin (`Net Margin` vs `FCF Margin`), giving six factor series. They are kept as two clean bridges rather than one fabricated 6-way bridge, because EPS and FCF/S are different outputs and would not tie out to a single quantity. Full per-period values and both sets of absolute-share weights are in `outputs/phase7/phase7_combined_six_factor_panel.csv`.

### Shared vs distinct factors

- `Rev/S` contribution is identical in both bridges every year (same revenue and share base), so revenue is a common engine for both EPS and FCF.
- Share-count contribution is also identical in both bridges (same diluted share base). Buybacks therefore lift EPS and FCF/S by the exact same log amount each year; neither output is structurally more share-count sensitive. What differs is which margin the buyback effect is paired with.
- The only genuinely distinct factors are `Net Margin` (EPS) and `FCF Margin` (FCF). Their log-contribution correlation over `2001-2025` is `0.4531` - they move together loosely but not tightly, because capex and working-capital timing push FCF margin around independently of reported net margin.

### Direct Answers (combined view)

- Revenue is the most consistent positive contributor across both EPS and FCF; over `2020-2025` it is positive in every period for each.
- The volatile factor in each bridge is the margin term; the FCF-margin term is the more volatile of the two and the only one that was a net negative over the recent five years.
- Margin is the dominant swing factor in EPS more often than in FCF on a weight basis in 2022-2024, while FCF leans more heavily on revenue; in 2024-2025 the two converge as FCF margin recovered.
- Because the share-count term is shared and small (2-19% weight), buyback timing matters equally to both metrics, but it is never the lead factor outside the 2013-2014 break year.
